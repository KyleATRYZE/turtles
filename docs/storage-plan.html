The storage room

A proposal for the sorted chest store in the factory: what goes where, how things get sorted away, and how you get them back out. Built from what the swarm has in stock right now.

The layout

The storage room is the middle room of the ground floor, with the smelting hall to the west and the launch bay to the east. Doorways in the middle of both long walls stay clear. The drone lane runs along the north end behind its screen, so no drone ever comes into the room, and the ladder to the upper floor is in the south-east corner.

  • Double chests down both long walls, three high. Each colour is one section, so everything of a kind is in one place and easy to find by eye too.
  • Wired modems hidden behind the chests, one per double chest, in a strip against each wall. Cable under the floor joins both walls, the desk and crafter1. Every chest is on the network, but you only ever see the chests.
  • The front desk at the south end: an In chest, an Out chest, and the sorting computer (Sortnado) between them, with a 5 × 3 touch screen on the wall above.
  • The same network joins crafter1's. The factory and mapper1 take from, and put into, these chests through the same sorting rules, so nothing gets scattered.

How it works

Putting things away

  1. Drop anything in the In chest. Miners' loads and the factory's leftovers arrive the same way.
  2. Sortnado looks at each stack, works out its section (from the item's name and tags) and tops up a chest that already holds that item. Failing that, it uses the first chest in that section with room.
  3. A full section spills into Overflow, and the screen says which section wants more room. Anything it has never seen before also goes to Overflow until it's given a home.
  4. Big piles are packed into storage blocks when space gets tight: redstone, raw ores, lapis, compressed cobble and dirt. crafter1 already does this, and unpacks them again when it needs the items.

Getting things out

  • On the screen: type a few letters on the touch keyboard, tap the item, tap 16, 64 or All. It lands in the Out chest.
  • From anywhere, in chat: $get diamond 32 puts it in the Out chest and CommandBlock tells you when it's ready.
  • Sortnado keeps an index of where everything is, so a request takes about a second, not a full search of 120 chests.

The touch screen

Drawn by the real screen program in CC's own font, with this monitor's own palette matching the section colours. Tap a section to browse it; the keyboard narrows the list as you type.

Under the hood

No turtles carry anything in here. Every chest on the wired network shows up to the computer as a peripheral, and CC can move items straight from one chest to another with pushItems. Sortnado is just an advanced computer doing bookkeeping.

  1. The index. On start-up it reads every chest once with list() and builds a table: for each item, which chest and slot holds it and how many. It also counts free slots per chest.
  2. Sorting. Once a second it checks the In chest. Each stack is matched to a section by name (plus item tags, looked up once per new item and cached). It goes to a part-filled stack of the same item in that section first, then to an empty slot there, then to Overflow. The index is updated as it goes.
  3. Requests. A tap on the screen, $get in chat (through the Chat Box), or a rednet message from crafter1 or mapper1. The index says where the item is, and pushItems sends it to the Out chest or the drone's loading chest.
  4. Keeping honest. Everything normally goes through Sortnado, so the index stays right. A slow re-check of a few chests every minute catches anything a person put in by hand.

Speed. Each move takes one game tick, and Sortnado sends several at once. A full In chest is put away in about two seconds, and a request arrives in about one.

crafter1 stops scanning every chest itself. It asks Sortnado for what a recipe needs and hands its products back through the same rules, so the store and the factory never disagree.

The core of it

-- put away everything in the In chest
for slot, stack in pairs(inbox.list()) do
  local sec  = sectionOf(stack.name)
  local dest = partStack(sec, stack.name)
            or emptySlot(sec)
            or emptySlot("Overflow")
  local moved = inbox.pushItems(dest.chest,
                  slot, stack.count, dest.slot)
  index:add(stack.name, dest, moved)
end

-- a request: "64 diamond to the Out chest"
local need = 64
for _, at in ipairs(index:find("minecraft:diamond")) do
  local got = peripheral.call(at.chest, "pushItems",
                outbox, at.slot, need)
  index:take(at, got)
  need = need - got
  if need == 0 then break end
end

Room to grow

ATM10 has so many items that the store has to be able to grow without being rebuilt. It can, in three ways, and Sortnado never needs to change: to CC an upgraded chest is just a chest with more slots.

  1. Upgrade the chests where they stand. Sophisticated Storage is in the pack. A Basic Tier Upgrade turns a vanilla chest into a Sophisticated one with everything still inside, and tier kits take it up from there, also in place. The table shows what the same 120 chests hold at each tier.
  2. Stack Upgrades go in a Sophisticated chest's upgrade slots and multiply how much each slot holds. They stack with each other, so the bulk sections can hold many times the table's figures.
  3. A drawer wall for the mountains. Functional Storage is in the pack too. A few drawers take the handful of items we have thousands of (cobble, deepslate, redstone), which leaves the chests for the long tail of everything else.

When there's power, AE2 or Refined Storage can take over the bulk, and Advanced Peripherals' ME and RS Bridges let Sortnado and the screen keep working on top.

What it takes

Materials

  • 120 chests: 960 planks, so about 240 logs. That's the tree farm's first big job, so the walls fill up a section at a time as wood comes in.
  • 60 full wired modems and about 60 cable: stone and redstone, all in stock.
  • 1 advanced computer, 15 advanced monitors, 2 chests for the front desk.

Order

  1. Fit-out: mapper1 places the modem strips, cable runs and front desk, then the chests a section at a time as logs allow.
  2. Sortnado's program: sorting, the index, the screen, $get.
  3. crafter1 switches to the same rules, so the factory and the store agree.
  4. The chests by the hut are emptied through the In chest, then join the walls.
